# Mastering Google Sheets for Data Analytics

## Introduction
While Python and SQL are the powerhouses of data science, Google Sheets remains the primary tool for rapid data exploration, cleaning, and ad-hoc reporting in professional environments. Mastering Sheets is essential for the **Prepare** and **Process** phases of the data analytics lifecycle, especially when dealing with smaller datasets or collaborating with non-technical stakeholders.

---

## 1. Core Analytical Capabilities
Google Sheets is more than a grid; it is a functional tool for performing initial data analysis before committing to complex Python scripts.

### Key Features for Analysts:
* **Data Cleaning:** Using tools like *Remove Duplicates*, *Find and Replace*, and *Split text to columns* to clean messy data imports quickly.
* **Conditional Formatting:** Highlighting trends, outliers, or specific thresholds (e.g., flagging sales figures below a target) to make data immediately readable.
* **Pivot Tables:** The bread and butter of analytical reporting. Use these to aggregate thousands of rows into actionable summaries (Sum, Count, Average) without writing a single line of code.
* **Lookups & Joins:** Mastering `VLOOKUP`, `HLOOKUP`, and the modern `XLOOKUP` to merge datasets—a concept analogous to SQL `JOIN` operations.

---

## 2. Advanced Functions and Automation
To move beyond basic data entry, you must leverage Sheets' powerful analytical functions.

* **Statistical Functions:** `AVERAGEIF`, `SUMIFS`, `COUNTIFS`, and `STDEV.P` to perform baseline descriptive statistics.
* **Logical Functions:** `IF`, `IFS`, `AND`, `OR`, and `NOT` for creating complex categorizations (e.g., classifying customers as 'High-Value' vs 'Low-Value' based on spend).
* **Array Formulas:** Using `ARRAYFORMULA()` to apply a calculation to an entire column at once, which is significantly more efficient than dragging formulas down thousands of rows.

---

## 3. Visualisation and Reporting
Sheets offers immediate visual feedback that is invaluable for the **Share** phase of your analysis.

* **Chart Types:** Knowing when to use a Bar Chart (categorical comparisons), Line Chart (time-series trends), or Scatter Plot (identifying correlations between two variables).
* **Sparklines:** Using the `=SPARKLINE()` function to create mini-charts directly inside a cell—perfect for a quick, "at-a-glance" dashboard.
* **Slicers & Filters:** Creating interactive reports that allow stakeholders to filter data dynamically, improving the user experience for non-analysts.

---

## 4. The Analyst’s Best Practices
To keep your Sheets professional and reproducible:
1. **Never Edit Raw Data:** Always keep a "Raw Data" tab. Create a secondary "Cleaned Data" tab to perform your transformations. 
2. **Document Your Work:** Use clear headers and colour-code your tabs. If you use a complex formula, add a text note explaining what it does.
3. **Optimise for Performance:** Too many complex formulas in a single sheet can lead to slow load times. If a sheet becomes sluggish, it’s usually a signal to move that part of your workflow into SQL or Python.
4. **Version Control:** If you are working on a shared business file, use the "Version History" feature to track who made changes and when.
